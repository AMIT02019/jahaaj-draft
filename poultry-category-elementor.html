Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)

"When Feed Costs Increase, Profits Shrink"

Feed is the single largest expense in poultry production, accounting for 60-80% of total production costs. Even a small increase in FCR translates into tonnes of additional feed consumption over a production cycle. Our nutritional solutions are designed to improve feed utilization, helping birds grow efficiently while reducing feed wastage and production costs.

Gut Health

"A Weak Gut Costs More Than You Think"

When gut health is compromised, birds absorb fewer nutrients from the same amount of feed, leading to slower growth, inconsistent flock uniformity, reduced immunity, and increased medication costs. Maintaining a healthy digestive system allows birds to make better use of every gram of feed while building stronger natural defences.

Loose Droppings & Wet Litter

"Wet Litter Doesn't Just Affect the Floor—It Affects Your Bottom Line"

Excess moisture increases ammonia production, damages footpads, reduces bird comfort, promotes bacterial growth, and raises the risk of myiasis and respiratory diseases. Supporting digestive efficiency helps produce firmer droppings, healthier litter, and a more productive growing environment.

Mycotoxin Management

"The Most Expensive Feed Problem Is the One You Can't See"

Mycotoxins contaminate up to 60% of crops worldwide each year and can significantly reduce feed efficiency, suppress immunity, damage the liver, impair reproduction, and increase mortality without obvious early warning signs. Protecting birds from hidden feed contaminants helps preserve performance and reduce avoidable economic losses.

Egg Production

"Every Egg Lost Is Revenue You Never Recover"

A small decline in laying percentage quickly becomes a substantial financial loss across thousands of birds. Nutritional deficiencies, oxidative stress, poor gut health, and metabolic challenges can all reduce laying persistence. Supporting reproductive health helps maintain egg production and flock uniformity.

Male Fertility

"Hatchability Starts Long Before the Hatchery"

Every infertile egg represents lost production potential. Poor semen quality and reduced fertility decrease hatchability, increase production costs, and lower the number of healthy chicks. Providing targeted nutritional support helps maintain breeder performance and improve reproductive efficiency.

Egg Yolk Pigmentation

"Consumers Judge Quality Before They Taste It"

The first thing consumers notice is appearance. Pale or uneven yolks create a perception of poor quality, reducing consumer confidence. Consistent yolk pigmentation helps deliver the visual quality consumers expect while strengthening brand value.

Kidney Health & Gout

"Hidden Kidney Stress Can Become a Costly Problem"

Kidney damage develops gradually before visible clinical signs appear. As kidney function declines, birds become more susceptible to gout, dehydration, reduced growth, and mortality. Supporting kidney health maintains productivity while reducing losses from metabolic disorders.

Early Chick Nutrition

"Build Strong Birds from Day One"

Early feed intake and intestinal development influence lifetime growth, feed efficiency, and immune competence. Providing targeted nutritional support during the first week builds stronger, healthier birds capable of reaching their full genetic potential.

Ammonia Management

"You Can't Improve Performance Without Improving the Environment"

High ammonia levels damage the respiratory tract, reduce feed intake, increase stress, and compromise immunity. Creating a cleaner environment supports healthier birds, better welfare, and improved production efficiency.

Fly Management

"Small Pests. Big Production Losses."

Flies are efficient carriers of pathogens that compromise biosecurity, stress birds, and create sanitation issues. Effective fly management contributes to healthier flocks and cleaner production facilities.

Vaccine Dye Indicator

"A Missed Bird Today Can Become Tomorrow's Outbreak"

Poor visibility during vaccination can lead to inconsistent application, leaving parts of the flock unprotected. Clear vaccine identification helps improve administration accuracy and ensures more uniform flock protection.
